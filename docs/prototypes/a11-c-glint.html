Middlemarch, A Study of Provincial Life, is set in a fictitious Midlands town from 1829 to 1832. It follows distinct, intersecting stories with many characters: Dorothea Brooke, a young idealist who marries the elderly scholar Casaubon; Tertius Lydgate, an ambitious doctor; Fred Vincy and Mary Garth; the banker Bulstrode.

The novel takes up the status of women, the nature of marriage, idealism, self-interest, religion, hypocrisy, political reform and education. Eliot uses a broad canvas and an omniscient narrator to draw the town as a web of lives.

It was first published in eight instalments in 1871 and 1872. Virginia Woolf called it one of the few English novels written for grown-up people.
