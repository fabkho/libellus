The mark in motion

The lamp rises behind the open book and its light fills the room, then everything rests on the static icon. It is one quiet movement, about a second and a half, made only of the motion tokens, and it is an SVG of a few kilobytes that plays by itself wherever an image can go.

Can Android's launch screen animate?

Not the screen Android draws, no. When an installed PWA starts, Android (and Chrome, for its WebAPK) draws the launch screen itself from the manifest: background_color, one of the manifest's icons and, before Android 12, the name. It is a static picture and the manifest has no field for motion. The animated launch icons Android 12 offers (an animated vector drawable in the SplashScreen API) are for native apps that ship that resource; a WebAPK cannot, and an animated SVG icon is turned into a still bitmap when the app is installed.

But the page can take over from it. The first thing the app paints can be the same picture: the night room (BetterReads' manifest already uses surface.dark, #0e0c0a) with the icon in the same place. To the eye the static splash simply comes alive, then gives way to the app. That is what the phone below plays.

  1. 1 · Android's launch screen. Static, drawn by the system from the manifest: the night room and the icon in a circle. Nothing here can move.
  2. 2 · The app's first paint, the same frame. The page draws the identical picture (inline in index.html, no JavaScript needed), so the handover is invisible, and the lamp's light swells out of the icon into the room over wave.
  3. 3 · The app. The launch picture leaves over exit and the app fades in over standard. It never waits for the animation: if the app is ready sooner, it cuts the swell short.
  • Only on a cold start of the installed app (display-mode: standalone), not on every navigation or in the browser.
  • With Reduce Motion it is a plain crossfade from the launch picture to the app.
  • The icon's size and place on Android's splash differ by Android version and maker (a circle in the middle since Android 12, the icon and the name before), so the takeover has to be measured on a real phone and tuned to Android 12+; on older ones it can only come close.
  • The full rise (the lamp coming up from behind the pages) belongs where nothing is waiting: the sign-in screen ("the way in", above the wordmark) and the README hero. On the launch screen the lamp is already up, because Android drew it that way.
